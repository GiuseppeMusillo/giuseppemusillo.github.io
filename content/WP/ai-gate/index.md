abstract: |
  How does generative AI reshape competition and production in markets for information? We study this question through Google's AI Overviews, which place generated answers above search results. We combine the US and UK launches with a pre-launch measure of content replaceability: the share of an outlet's articles containing neither original reporting nor a distinctive authorial voice. The measure is constructed from 31,232 archived articles. In a weekly panel of 67 outlets, a one-standard-deviation increase in replaceability is associated with a 10.8% larger decline in visits after launch and an 8.7% decline in monthly unique visitors. The traffic gap widens over the following two years, although pre-launch trends and concurrent changes in search limit attribution. Headline-based searches show that more replaceable outlets are less likely to be cited by AI Overviews, despite similar content overlap. We find no detectable differential adjustment in article output or content composition. A dynamic model illustrates how an improvement in an AI alternative can reduce demand and firm values across incumbents while imposing larger losses on less differentiated outlets. The findings point to content differentiation and platform attribution as distinct influences on the distribution of losses from generative search.
